# Planning, Governance, and Legal Awareness

> DevsLibrary · Web Accessibility · Lesson 35

## Accessibility needs ownership
Sustainable accessibility requires design, engineering, content, QA, product, procurement, and leadership to share responsibility. Assign owners for standards interpretation, component patterns, audits, defect triage, training, and release decisions.

## Shift left without stopping at design
Include access needs in research plans, acceptance criteria, design reviews, pull requests, test plans, procurement, and incident response. Use a checklist as a memory aid, not as proof that a complex experience is accessible.

## Conformance and law
Legal requirements vary by jurisdiction, sector, audience, and contract and may change over time. WCAG is a technical standard often referenced by policies, but legal compliance should be evaluated against current applicable requirements with qualified advice when needed. Do not give a blanket legal guarantee based on a tool score or one audit.

## Statement and feedback channel
An accessibility statement should be accurate, current, and specific about the scope evaluated, standards targeted, known limitations, and a contact method. Do not claim complete conformance if important flows were not tested. Make it easy for users to report barriers and provide a response process that actually works.

## Exercise
Draft an accessibility workstream for a small team: ownership, release gates, testing cadence, severity definitions, vendor checks, training, and a process for handling user feedback.
