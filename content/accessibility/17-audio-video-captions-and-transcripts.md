# Audio, Video, Captions, and Transcripts

> DevsLibrary · Web Accessibility · Lesson 17

## Match the alternative to the media
Prerecorded video with meaningful audio generally needs synchronized captions. Audio-only content needs a transcript. Prerecorded video may need audio description when important visual information is not conveyed by the soundtrack. Live media has additional requirements and operational constraints; check the applicable WCAG version and conformance target.

Captions should include spoken dialogue and relevant non-speech audio cues. They need accurate timing, readable presentation, and speaker identification where it matters. Auto-generated captions are a draft, not a guarantee of accuracy.

## Player controls
Media controls must be keyboard accessible, named, and understandable. Users need control over playback, volume, captions, and other available settings. Avoid autoplaying audio; if media starts automatically and lasts more than a brief period, users need a way to pause, stop, or control volume according to the applicable criterion.

## Audio description and transcripts
A transcript should include meaningful dialogue and sound information. Audio description communicates important visual actions, scene changes, text on screen, and other information not already available in audio. Plan for these alternatives during production rather than after publishing.

## Exercise
Audit a video lesson: check caption accuracy, speaker labels, control names, keyboard access, playback behavior, and whether visually important content is described in audio or an equivalent alternative.
